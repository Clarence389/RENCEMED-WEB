Clinical framework

What clinicians assess

This page organizes the major clinical decision points for Obesity. It is designed for patient education and professional revision, not as a substitute for a current guideline, local protocol or individualized clinical assessment.

Diagnosis / assessment
Diagnostic Domain Clinical Parameters & Thresholds Diagnostic Workup & Risk Stratification
Body Mass Index (BMI) Classification
  • Overweight: BMI 25.0 – 29.9 kg/m² (Asian populations: 23.0 – 24.9 kg/m²).
  • Class I Obesity: BMI 30.0 – 34.9 kg/m² (Asian populations: 25.0 – 29.9 kg/m²).
  • Class II Obesity: BMI 35.0 – 39.9 kg/m².
  • Class III (Severe/Morbid) Obesity: BMI ≥ 40.0 kg/m².
  • Calculate BMI using accurately measured height and weight (Weight [kg] / Height [m]²).
  • Use Edmonton Obesity Staging System (EOSS) to evaluate disease burden beyond BMI alone.
Waist Circumference & Visceral Risk
  • High Cardiovascular/Metabolic Risk Cut-offs:
    • Men: > 102 cm (> 40 inches); Asian men: > 90 cm (> 35 inches).
    • Women: > 88 cm (> 35 inches); Asian women: > 80 cm (> 31 inches).
  • Measure waist circumference at the midpoint between the lower rib margin and the iliac crest.
  • Provides crucial independent assessment of visceral fat distribution, particularly in individuals with BMI 25–35 kg/m².
Comorbidity & Laboratory Screening
  • Cardiometabolic: Type 2 Diabetes, impaired fasting glucose, dyslipidemia, HTN, Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD/NAFLD).
  • Mechanical/Other: Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), osteoarthritis, GERD, PCOS, and depression.
  • Screening Battery: Order Fasting Plasma Glucose / HbA1c, Fasting Lipid Profile, Liver Enzymes (ALT/AST), Serum Creatinine/eGFR, and Uric Acid.
  • Perform clinical screening for OSA (STOP-Bang questionnaire) and thyroid dysfunction (TSH).
Secondary Etiology Assessment
  • Evaluate for underlying endocrine or iatrogenic causes of weight gain (e.g., Cushing's syndrome, hypothyroidism, drug-induced weight gain).
  • Review current medications (e.g., antipsychotics, beta-blockers, sulfonylureas, insulin, corticosteroids, gabapentinoids).
  • Order targeted testing (e.g., 1mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test) if Cushingoid features are present.
Key monitoring
Monitoring Parameter Clinical Indicators & Target Goals Evaluation Frequency & Protocol
Weight Trend & Anthropometrics
  • Initial Weight Loss Goal: 5% – 10% reduction from baseline body weight over 6 months.
  • Waist Circumference: Track reduction in abdominal fat mass (target: < 102 cm for men, < 88 cm for women).
  • Measure weight and calculate BMI at every clinical visit (at least monthly during active weight loss, then q3–6 months during maintenance).
  • Re-evaluate waist circumference every 3–6 months.
Blood Pressure (BP) Surveillance
  • Clinical Target: BP < 130/80 mmHg.
  • Medication Deprescribing: Monitor for hypotension as weight loss lowers endogenous vascular resistance and improves insulin sensitivity.
  • Check blood pressure at every office visit.
  • Encourage home BP monitoring 2–3 times per week, adjusting antihypertensive dosages as weight loss progresses.
Glycemic & Lipid Biomarkers
  • Glycemic Goals: HbA1c < 5.7% (normal) or < 7.0% in patients with established T2D.
  • Lipid Profile Targets: Normalization of triglycerides (< 150 mg/dL), elevation of HDL-C, and reduction of LDL-C to risk-based targets.
  • Measure fasting glucose and HbA1c every 3–6 months until stable.
  • Check fasting lipid panel baseline, at 3–6 months post-intervention, and annually thereafter.
Treatment Efficacy & Medication Safety
  • Anti-Obesity Medication (AOM) Response Criteria: Benchmark loss of ≥ 5% total body weight within 12–16 weeks on therapeutic dosage.
  • Safety & Side Effects: Gastrointestinal adverse effects (GLP-1 RAs/GIP), pulse rate changes, or gallstone formation symptoms during rapid weight reduction.
  • Formally assess AOM response at 12–16 weeks; discontinue or switch therapy if < 5% weight loss is achieved.
  • Monitor renal function (eGFR/BUN) and liver panel (ALT/AST) every 6–12 months.
Management principles
Management Pillar Core Therapeutic Strategies Implementation Guidelines
Nutritional Therapy
  • Caloric Deficit: Create an energy deficit of 500–750 kcal/day (aim for 1200–1500 kcal/day for women, 1500–1800 kcal/day for men).
  • Dietary Patterns: Emphasize Mediterranean, DASH, or high-fiber low-glycemic eating patterns.
  • Focus on sustainable whole-food dietary changes rather than short-term restrictive diets.
  • Reduce ultra-processed foods, refined carbohydrates, and sugar-sweetened beverages.
Physical Activity & Exercise
  • Aerobic Exercise: Target 150–300 minutes/week of moderate-intensity (or 75–150 mins of vigorous) exercise for weight loss maintenance.
  • Resistance Training: Perform muscle-strengthening exercises 2–3 days per week to preserve lean body mass.
  • Gradually increase activity levels to avoid injury; advocate reducing sedentary screen time.
  • Tailor modalities to patient mobility and physical tolerance.
Behavioral & Lifestyle Counseling
  • Cognitive Behavioral Techniques: Self-monitoring (food/activity logs), stimulus control, goal setting (SMART targets), and stress management.
  • Psychological Support: Address emotional eating, binge eating disorder, and sleep hygiene.
  • Incorporate motivational interviewing to foster intrinsic motivation and long-term adherence.
  • Refer to behavioral health specialists or registered dietitians when indicated.
Pharmacotherapy & Bariatric Surgery
  • Pharmacotherapy (BMI ≥ 30, or ≥ 27 with comorbidities): GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists (e.g., Semaglutide, Tirzepatide), Orlistat, or Phentermine/Topiramate as adjuncts to lifestyle changes.
  • Metabolic/Bariatric Surgery (BMI ≥ 40, or ≥ 35 with severe comorbidities): Sleeve gastrectomy or Roux-en-Y gastric bypass.
  • Select pharmacotherapy based on patient comorbidities, drug interaction potential, and cost/access.
  • Ensure comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation before and after bariatric surgical procedures.
Follow-up / outcomes
Follow-up Domain Outcome Indicators & Clinical Targets Longitudinal Care Strategy
Weight Trajectory & Maintenance
  • Response Benchmark: Achieve ≥ 5% total weight loss at 3–6 months.
  • Long-Term Maintenance: Prevent weight regain after initial reduction plateau.
  • Schedule monthly visits during active loss phase, then every 3–6 months once stable.
  • If weight loss is < 5% at 12–16 weeks on full-dose pharmacotherapy, re-evaluate adherence, step up therapy, or switch agents.
Cardiometabolic Risk Reduction
  • Glycemic & BP Control: Improvement in HbA1c, fasting glucose, and blood pressure readings.
  • Lipids & Liver Markers: Normalization of TG/HDL ratio and reduction in hepatic steatosis markers (ALT/AST).
  • Recheck fasting lipids, HbA1c, and metabolic panels every 6–12 months.
  • Deprescribe or reduce dosages of antihypertensive and antidiabetic drugs as metabolic metrics improve.
Adverse Effects Surveillance
  • Pharmacotherapy Side Effects: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea/constipation (GLP-1/GIP RAs), steatorrhea (Orlistat), insomnia/heart rate elevation (Phentermine).
  • Surgical Complications: Micronutrient deficiencies (B12, Iron, Vitamin D), dumping syndrome, or cholelithiasis.
  • Assess medication tolerability at each dose titration step.
  • Perform routine post-bariatric nutritional screening (ferritin, B12, folate, 25-OH Vitamin D, calcium) every 6–12 months.
Adherence & Relapse Prevention
  • Lifestyle Consistency: Ongoing tracking of dietary habits, daily step count, and physical activity logs.
  • Weight Plateau Management: Address metabolic adaptation and emotional or stress-related behavioral lapses.
  • Use non-judgmental counseling to address non-adherence and barriers to lifestyle consistency.
  • Reassure patient that weight plateau is a physiological response and reinforce maintenance strategies.
Patient education
Education Domain Core Patient Counseling Messages Verification & Action Plan
Disease Understanding & Goals
  • Chronic Disease Nature: Reframe obesity as a complex, chronic, relapsing metabolic condition rather than a personal failure.
  • Treatment Purpose: Explain that modest weight loss (5%–10%) significantly improves blood pressure, glucose control, and joint pain.
  • Emphasize health outcomes and functional capacity over purely cosmetic weight targets.
  • Set realistic expectations regarding weight plateaus and long-term maintenance.
Lifestyle Modification & Adherence
  • Nutrition & Activity: Guide small, sustainable dietary adjustments and gradual increases in routine physical activity.
  • Medication Adherence: Emphasize continuous, long-term use of anti-obesity pharmacotherapy as prescribed, avoiding abrupt cessation.
  • Encourage daily or weekly self-weighing and dietary journaling for self-awareness.
  • Counsel on correct subcutaneous injection technique and dose escalation schedules for GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists.
Urgent Care Warnings & Red Flags
  • Severe Gastrointestinal Warning Signs: Instruct patient to seek immediate medical care if experiencing:
    • Severe, persistent abdominal pain radiating to the back (pancreatitis risk).
    • Intractable vomiting leading to dehydration.
    • Right upper quadrant pain, fever, or jaundice (gallstones/cholecystitis risk).
  • Provide clear contact information for clinic triage and emergency protocols.
  • Educate post-bariatric patients on symptoms requiring urgent review (e.g., severe vomiting, signs of anastomotic leak or obstruction).
Teach-Back Confirmation Method
  • Understanding Check: Ensure the patient understands their medication schedule, dietary targets, and warning signs.
  • Ask the patient to explain in their own words:
    • "What is our primary weight loss goal for the next 3 to 6 months?"
    • "How will you manage common side effects like mild nausea at home?"
    • "Which symptoms mean you should call our office or visit urgent care immediately?"
Multidisciplinary roles
Team Discipline Core Responsibilities & Clinical Scope Key Interprofessional Deliverables
Physicians & Specialists
  • Assessment & Diagnosis: Conduct medical evaluations, screen for secondary causes, classify obesity severity, and treat comorbidities.
  • Therapeutic Decisions: Prescribe anti-obesity pharmacotherapy, oversee dose titration, and evaluate candidacy for bariatric surgery.
  • Establish comprehensive treatment plans and target percentage weight loss goals.
  • Coordinate referrals to bariatric surgeons, endocrinologists, and behavioral therapists.
Pharmacists
  • Medicine Optimization: Verify appropriate dosing and escalation schedules for anti-obesity agents (e.g., GLP-1 RAs).
  • Safety & Interaction Screening: Review concurrent medications for drug-induced weight gain and manage drug interactions.
  • Counsel patients on subcutaneous injection technique, storage, and managing gastrointestinal side effects.
  • Support adherence and evaluate potential cost/coverage barriers for anti-obesity agents.
Nurses & Allied Health (Dietitians/Exercise Specialists)
  • Surveillance & Monitoring: Track weight trends, waist circumference, blood pressure, and metabolic laboratory parameters.
  • Behavioral Coaching & Coordination: Provide structured dietary counseling, physical activity prescription, and lifestyle education.
  • Deliver structured behavioral interventions and reinforce lifestyle goals at every visit.
  • Maintain regular clinical touchpoints between specialist visits to maintain motivation.
Patient & Caregivers
  • Active Shared Decision-Making: Participate in setting realistic weight and lifestyle targets.
  • Self-Management: Maintain self-monitoring logs (food, steps, weight), take medications as agreed, and report problems promptly.
  • Consistently follow nutritional and physical activity plans.
  • Promptly report adverse medication effects or weight maintenance barriers to the care team.
Individualization
Patient Factor Clinical Impact & Vulnerabilities Tailoring Strategy & Regimen Adjustments
Age Extremes & Pregnancy
  • Older Adults (> 65 yrs): Increased risk of sarcopenia (muscle loss) and bone mineral density loss with rapid weight reduction.
  • Pregnancy & Lactation: Anti-obesity medications are contraindicated during pregnancy due to fetal risk.
  • In older adults, prioritize resistance training and high protein intake (≥ 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day) during weight loss; aim for moderate weight reduction.
  • Discontinue anti-obesity medications at least 2 months prior to planned conception and focus on controlled gestational weight gain.
Renal & Hepatic Impairment
  • Renal Impairment: Dehydration/volume depletion from GLP-1 RA-induced GI side effects can worsen acute kidney injury.
  • Hepatic Impairment: Altered clearance for certain oral centrally-acting anti-obesity agents.
  • Maintain adequate hydration when initiating GLP-1 RAs; dose-adjust or avoid Phentermine/Topiramate or Naltrexone/Bupropion in severe renal/hepatic impairment.
  • Monitor eGFR and liver function periodically during titration.
Comorbidities & Concomitant Meds
  • Weight-Gaining Drugs: Insulin, sulfonylureas, antipsychotics, antidepressants (e.g., mirtazapine, paroxetine), and beta-blockers exacerbate weight gain.
  • Cardiovascular / Mental Health: History of major depression, suicidal ideation, or uncontrolled hypertension.
  • Switch to weight-neutral or weight-reducing alternatives when feasible (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors or Metformin for T2D; SSRIs/SNRIs with lower weight impact).
  • Avoid Phentermine/Topiramate in uncontrolled HTN/CAD, and avoid Naltrexone/Bupropion in patients with seizure disorders or active opioid use.
Socioeconomic Barriers & Preferences
  • Financial Limits: High out-of-pocket costs and limited insurance coverage for novel GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists.
  • Lifestyle Realities: Food insecurity, lack of accessible exercise spaces, and cultural dietary preferences.
  • Consider lower-cost pharmacotherapy options (e.g., Metformin off-label, Orlistat, generic formulations) when modern AOMs are inaccessible.
  • Tailor dietary recommendations to culturally relevant whole foods and regional food availability.

Patient Checklist

  • Know the name and purpose of each medicine.
  • Know how and when to take or use treatment.
  • Ask what adverse effects require medical attention.
  • Keep appointments and monitoring tests.
  • Tell the healthcare team about OTC medicines, supplements and allergies.
  • Do not stop long-term treatment abruptly without professional advice unless an emergency clinician instructs otherwise.
