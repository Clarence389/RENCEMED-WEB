Clinical framework

What clinicians assess

This page organizes the major clinical decision points for Stroke. It is designed for patient education and professional revision, not as a substitute for a current guideline, local protocol or individualized clinical assessment.

Diagnosis / assessment
Clinical Assessment Step Diagnostic Criteria & Methodologies Clinical Purpose & Target Findings
Rapid Clinical Triage & Onset Determination
  • BE-FAST / FAST Tool: Balance, Eyes (vision loss), Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services.
  • LKW (Last Known Well): Establish exact time patient was last seen baseline normal to calculate reperfusion eligibility windows.
  • Emergency Fingerstick Glucose: Immediate blood glucose test prior to any intervention.
  • Rapidly identify hyperacute neurological deficits.
  • Rule out hypoglycemia as a stroke mimic (glucose < 3.0 mmol/L or < 55 mg/dL).
  • Determine candidate eligibility for intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) and mechanical thrombectomy (MT).
Neurological Severity & Deficit Scoring
  • NIHSS (NIH Stroke Scale): 11-item baseline quantitative scale (score 0–42).
    • Minor: 1–4
    • Moderate: 5–15
    • Moderate-Severe: 16–20
    • Severe: > 20
  • Quantify initial deficit severity and establish baseline for treatment monitoring.
  • Guide decision-making for reperfusion therapy (e.g., NIHSS ≥ 6 suggests large vessel occlusion).
Emergency Neuroimaging & Angiography
  • Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Brain: Urgent immediate imaging upon hospital arrival.
  • CTA (CT Angiography) / MRA: Aortic arch to vertex vascular imaging.
  • CT / MR Perfusion (CTP / MRP): Automated salvageable tissue mapping (ASPECTS score / ischemic core vs. penumbra).
  • Differentiate ischemic stroke from acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICH).
  • Identify Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) in ICA, MCA, or Basilar artery.
  • Extend thrombectomy window up to 24 hours in selected LVO cases (DAWN / DEFUSE-3 criteria).
Etiologic Workup & Baseline Diagnostics
  • Cardiovascular Workup: 12-lead ECG, telemetry monitoring (> 24h), and Transthoracic / Transesophageal Echocardiography (TTE/TEE).
  • Baseline Labs: FBC, Coagulation panel (INR, aPTT), Renal function, Electrolytes, Troponin, Lipid panel, HbA1c.
  • TOAST Classification: Categorize cause into Large-artery atherosclerosis, Cardioembolism, Small-vessel occlusion (lacunar), Other, or Undetermined.
  • Detect occult Atrial Fibrillation or structural cardiac sources of emboli.
  • Identify underlying atherosclerotic disease, carotid stenosis, or microvascular pathology to guide secondary prevention.
Key monitoring
Monitoring Parameter Target Range & Frequency Clinical Purpose & Action Thresholds
Neurological Checks & NIHSS Scoring
  • Post-Thrombolysis/Thrombectomy: Every 15 mins for 2 hours, then q30m for 6 hours, then q1h for 16 hours.
  • Routine Ward Care: Every 2–4 hours during acute phase (< 72 hours).
  • Detect early neurological deterioration (≥ 4 point increase in NIHSS).
  • Identify symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) or cerebral edema/herniation.
  • Trigger urgent repeat non-contrast CT head if neuro decline occurs.
Blood Pressure Control
  • Post-Thrombolysis (IVT): Maintain BP < 180/105 mmHg for at least 24 hours.
  • Ischemic (No IVT/MT): Allow permissive hypertension up to < 220/120 mmHg for first 24–48 hours.
  • Hemorrhagic (ICH): Rapid lowering to target SBP 130–140 mmHg.
  • Prevent reperfusion injury, arterial rupture, and hemorrhagic transformation.
  • Maintain penumbral perfusion in non-thrombolyzed acute ischemic stroke.
  • Utilize IV Labetalol, Nicardipine, or Clevidipine for titration.
Blood Glucose & Body Temperature
  • Blood Glucose Target: 7.8–10.0 mmol/L (140–180 mg/dL); check q1–4h.
  • Core Temperature Target: Normothermia (< 37.5°C / 99.5°F); check q4h.
  • Avoid hypoglycemia (< 3.9 mmol/L) which causes acute neuro worsening.
  • Prevent severe hyperglycemia which accelerates ischemic tissue infarction.
  • Treat fever promptly with paracetamol/cooling (hyperthermia increases metabolic penumbral injury).
Swallow Screening & Complications
  • Dysphagia Screen: Formal validated bedside water swallow test prior to any oral intake, meds, or hydration.
  • Complication Monitoring: Daily assessment for aspiration pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), urinary retention, and pressure injury.
  • Prevent aspiration pneumonia (major cause of early post-stroke mortality).
  • Place NG tube if unsafe swallow persists past 24–48 hours.
  • Initiate early intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) devices for DVT prophylaxis.
Management principles
Management Domain Intervention Protocol & Regimen Clinical Rationale & Eligibility Criteria
Hyperacute Reperfusion Therapy
  • Intravenous Thrombolysis (IVT): Alteplase (0.9 mg/kg IV max 90 mg; 10% bolus, remainder over 60 min) OR Tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg single bolus max 25 mg) within 0–4.5 hours of onset.
  • Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT): Mechanical retrieval within 0–6 hours (up to 24 hours with penumbral mismatch on CTP/MRP).
  • Restore cerebral blood flow and salvage ischemic penumbra.
  • Key Contraindications for IVT: Active internal bleeding, INR > 1.7, platelets < 100k, severe head trauma within 3 months, or BP > 185/110 mmHg.
  • EVT is indicated for anterior circulation Large Vessel Occlusions (LVOs) with pre-stroke mRS 0–1.
Acute Medical & Antithrombotic Care
  • Non-Thrombolyzed Ischemic: Aspirin 160–325 mg immediately.
  • Post-Thrombolysis: Delay antiplatelets for 24 hours until repeat CT excludes hemorrhage.
  • Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT): Aspirin + Clopidogrel (75 mg daily) for 21–90 days in minor stroke (NIHSS ≤ 3) or high-risk TIA (ABCD2 ≥ 4).
  • Prevent early recurrent ischemic stroke and early arterial re-occlusion.
  • Cardioembolic stroke (e.g., Atrial Fibrillation): Start DOAC / Warfarin 4–14 days post-onset based on stroke size and hemorrhage risk (1-3-6-12 day rule).
Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH) Management
  • Anticoagulation Reversal: Immediate administration of reversal agents (Idarucizumab for Dabigatran, Andexanet alfa or PCC for Factor Xa inhibitors, 4-factor PCC + Vitamin K for Warfarin).
  • Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Control: Elevate head of bed to 30°, hypertonic saline / mannitol, and neurosurgical consult for decompression or EVD placement.
  • Halt hematoma expansion (primary determinant of early mortality).
  • Target rapid blood pressure lowering (SBP 130–140 mmHg within 2 hours).
  • Surgical evacuation indicated for cerebellar hemorrhage > 3 cm or lobar ICH with rapid decline.
Stroke Unit Care & Early Rehab
  • Dedicated Stroke Unit: Admission to a geographic multi-disciplinary stroke unit within 24 hours.
  • Early Rehabilitation: Out-of-bed mobilization within 24–48 hours (avoid intense mobilization in first 24h).
  • Supportive Measures: Hydration via IV/NG, glycemic control, euthermia, and IPC for VTE prophylaxis.
  • Dedicated stroke unit care reduces 1-year mortality and dependency by 20%.
  • Prevents secondary medical complications (aspiration, pressure sores, joint contractures, venous thromboembolism).
Follow-up / outcomes
Outcome Domain Evaluation Parameters & Monitoring Schedule Target Outcomes & Secondary Prevention Goals
Functional Recovery & Disability Scoring
  • Modified Rankin Scale (mRS): Evaluate at 90 days post-stroke (0 = no symptoms, 1–2 = slight disability, 3–5 = moderate-to-severe disability, 6 = dead).
  • Barthel Index (BI): Assess performance in activities of daily living (ADLs) at discharge, 3 months, and 12 months.
  • Target: Achieve functional independence (mRS 0–2).
  • Action: Continue community outpatient neuro-rehabilitation, occupational therapy (OT), physical therapy (PT), and speech-language therapy.
Vascular Risk Factor Control
  • Blood Pressure: Office BP monitoring at every visit; target < 130/80 mmHg.
  • Lipid Profile: Serum LDL-C at 4–12 weeks post-stroke.
  • Glycemic Control: HbA1c screening; target < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) in patients with diabetes.
  • Target LDL-C: High-intensity statin therapy to reduce LDL-C to < 1.4 mmol/L (< 55 mg/dL) or ≥ 50% reduction for atherosclerotic stroke.
  • Action: Add Ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors if LDL-C target is not met on maximum tolerated statin dose.
Stroke Recurrence & Rhythm Surveillance
  • Extended Cardiac Monitoring: 30-day Holter / long-term insertable cardiac monitor (ICM) for cryptogenic ischemic stroke.
  • Carotid Duplex Ultrasound / CTA: Serial imaging for non-operated moderate-to-severe carotid stenosis.
  • Target: Zero recurrent ischemic events or hemorrhagic complications.
  • Action: Initiate DOAC therapy if paroxysmal Atrial Fibrillation is detected; schedule carotid endarterectomy (CEA) or stenting (CAS) for symptomatic 70–99% stenosis within 14 days of event.
Long-Term Complications & Quality of Life
  • Post-Stroke Depression (PSD): Screen using PHQ-9 at 1–3 months and annually.
  • Post-Stroke Spasticity & Pain: Clinical exam for limb contractures, shoulder subluxation, or central neuropathic pain.
  • Cognitive Impairment: MoCA / MMSE testing to screen for vascular cognitive impairment or dementia.
  • Target: Early detection and control of neuropsychiatric and musculoskeletal complications.
  • Action: Initiate SSRIs for PSD; utilize targeted botulinum toxin injections for focal spasticity; prescribe neuropathic agents (e.g., Gabapentin/Pregabalin) for post-stroke pain.
Patient education
Education Domain Core Clinical Explanation & Guidance Teach-Back Prompts & Verification
Diagnosis & Purpose of Treatment
  • Understanding Stroke: Explain that a stroke occurs when blood flow to a part of the brain is blocked (Ischemic) or a blood vessel bursts (Hemorrhagic), injuring brain cells.
  • Purpose of Therapies: Emphasize that acute medications work to clear blockages or control bleeding, while long-term preventive medicines prevent future blood clots and protect brain tissue.
  • Time-Critical Recovery: Reiterate that early rehabilitation and strict risk factor control give the best chance for physical and functional recovery.
  • "In your own words, can you explain what happened during your stroke and why taking your new daily preventive medication is necessary?"
  • "How do antiplatelet/blood thinner medications help protect you against a second stroke?"
Medication Adherence & Safety
  • Strict Daily Regimen: Emphasize that antiplatelet agents (Aspirin/Clopidogrel), blood thinners (DOACs/Warfarin), statins, and BP pills must be taken daily without missing doses.
  • Bleeding Precautions: Educate on signs of excess bleeding when taking blood thinners (unusual bruising, dark/tarry stools, blood in urine, bleeding gums).
  • OTC & Herbals: Warn against taking over-the-counter NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen) or herbal supplements without consulting the care team.
  • "What should you do if you miss a dose of your blood thinner or antiplatelet medication?"
  • "What are two signs of bleeding you should watch out for while taking your blood-thinning pills?"
Lifestyle Measures & Risk Modification
  • Diet & Blood Pressure: Recommend a low-sodium (< 2g/day), Mediterranean-style diet rich in vegetables, fruits, and whole grains to optimize BP and lipids.
  • Smoking & Alcohol Cessation: Advise complete smoking cessation and strict alcohol limitation to lower stroke recurrence risk.
  • Physical Activity & Rehabilitation: Encourage daily prescribed rehabilitation exercises and 150 mins/week of moderate physical activity as tolerated.
  • "What changes will you make to your daily diet to help keep your blood pressure within a healthy range?"
  • "How often do you plan to perform your physical therapy exercises at home each week?"
Monitoring & When to Seek Urgent Care
  • Home BP & Glucose Log: Instruct patient to keep a daily log of home blood pressure and blood glucose (if diabetic).
  • Recognizing Stroke Recurrence (BE-FAST):
    • Balance loss / Sudden dizziness
    • Eyes / Sudden vision changes
    • Facial drooping
    • Arm or leg weakness/numbness
    • Speech difficulty or slurring
    • Time to call Emergency Services IMMEDIATELY
  • Urgent Red Flags: Sudden severe headache, confusion, difficulty swallowing, or sudden weakness.
  • "If you notice sudden facial drooping, speech difficulty, or arm weakness again, what action will you take immediately?"
  • "Why is it dangerous to 'wait and see' if new stroke symptoms will go away on their own?"
Multidisciplinary roles
Team Member Primary Responsibilities Collaborative Focus & Care Delivery
Neurologists & Physicians
  • Assessment & Diagnosis: Perform urgent clinical evaluation, calculate NIHSS scores, order/interpret brain imaging (CT/CTA/MRI), and establish stroke subtype.
  • Treatment Decisions: Direct hyperacute reperfusion therapy (IV thrombolysis / endovascular thrombectomy), manage acute complications, and prescribe secondary prevention[cite: 2].
  • Lead hyperacute stroke response, coordinate with neurointerventional radiologists and neurosurgeons, and define overall recovery targets[cite: 2].
Clinical Pharmacists
  • Medication Optimization: Reconcile acute and secondary prevention drugs, evaluate thrombolytic eligibility/dosing, and check drug-drug interactions[cite: 2].
  • Adherence & Safety: Screen for contraindications (e.g., active bleeding risks, drug interactions with DOACs), optimize antiplatelet/anticoagulant timing, and support long-term adherence[cite: 2].
  • Monitor narrow therapeutic range agents (e.g., Warfarin INR), guide statin intensity titration, and counsel patients on medication safety[cite: 2].
Stroke & Rehabilitation Nurses
  • Monitoring & Surveillance: Perform frequent neurological assessments, track vitals (BP, glucose, temperature), and conduct bedside dysphagia screenings[cite: 2].
  • Patient Care & Education: Administer acute IV medications, deliver patient/family education, coordinate care, and prevent complications like pressure ulcers and aspiration[cite: 2].
  • Serve as frontline patient advocates, coordinate daily multidisciplinary rounds, and ensure safety during early post-stroke mobilization[cite: 2].
Physio, Occupational & Speech Therapists
  • Functional Rehabilitation: Physiotherapists restore mobility and gait; Occupational Therapists optimize ADLs and cognitive-motor tasks.
  • Swallow & Language Recovery: Speech-Language Pathologists assess formal dysphagia, manage aphasia/dysarthria, and recommend safe diet textures.
  • Collaborate to rebuild functional independence, prevent muscle contractures/aspiration, and structure community reintegration plans.
Patient & Support Network
  • Shared Decision-Making: Participate actively in treatment choices, rehabilitation goal setting, and secondary prevention plans[cite: 2].
  • Adherence & Reporting: Take prescribed medications as agreed, engage in daily therapy exercises, keep BP/glucose logs, and report red-flag symptoms promptly[cite: 2].
  • Adopt lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, smoking cessation) and serve as active partners in post-stroke recovery and recurrence prevention[cite: 2].
Individualization
Individualization Variable Clinical Assessment & Risk Modifiers Tailored Management Adjustments
Age & Frailty Factors
  • Elderly / Octogenarians (≥ 80 yrs): Elevated risk of hemorrhagic transformation, fall risk, and post-stroke delirium.
  • Pediatric / Young Stroke: Non-atherosclerotic etiologies (e.g., arterial dissection, hypercoagulable states, patent foramen ovale).
  • Age alone is NOT a contraindication for IV thrombolysis or thrombectomy; weigh benefit vs. bleeding risk carefully.
  • Tailor antithrombotic selection and dosage based on fall risk and renal decline.
Pregnancy & Postpartum State
  • Physiologic State: Hypercoagulability increases risk of cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST) and ischemic stroke.
  • Fetal Safety: Teratogenicity risks associated with standard secondary prevention drugs (e.g., Statins, Warfarin in 1st trimester, DOACs).
  • IV thrombolysis is relative; benefit usually outweighs risk in severe stroke.
  • Use Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH) or unfractionated heparin for anticoagulation; avoid statins and DOACs during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
Renal & Hepatic Function
  • Severe Renal Impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min): Impaired clearance of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) and contrast toxicity risk for CTA/CTP.
  • Hepatic Dysfunction: Coagulopathy increases baseline intracranial hemorrhage risk.
  • Dose-adjust or switch DOACs (or switch to Warfarin / LMWH) in severe renal failure.
  • Do not delay emergent unenhanced head CT or life-saving CTA for baseline creatinine results.
Allergies & Drug Interactions
  • Aspirin Hypersensitivity: Inability to tolerate standard first-line antiplatelet therapy.
  • Concurrent Anticoagulation: Active use of therapeutic anticoagulants (DOAC/Warfarin with INR > 1.7) at stroke presentation.
  • Substitute Clopidogrel 75 mg daily in aspirin-allergic patients.
  • IV thrombolysis is contraindicated if therapeutic anticoagulation was taken within 24–48 hours unless specific reversal agents or anti-Xa levels permit.
Socioeconomic Barriers & Preferences
  • Financial & Access Constraints: High cost of DOACs or access barriers to long-term outpatient rehabilitation.
  • Monitoring Capabilities: Inability to perform routine lab monitoring for Warfarin (INR).
  • Choose generic Warfarin when DOACs are unaffordable, provided regular INR monitoring is feasible.
  • Align rehabilitation modality (inpatient, home-based, or tele-rehab) with social support systems and insurance coverage.

Patient Checklist

  • Know the name and purpose of each medicine.
  • Know how and when to take or use treatment.
  • Ask what adverse effects require medical attention.
  • Keep appointments and monitoring tests.
  • Tell the healthcare team about OTC medicines, supplements and allergies.
  • Do not stop long-term treatment abruptly without professional advice unless an emergency clinician instructs otherwise.
