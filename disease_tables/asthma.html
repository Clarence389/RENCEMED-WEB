Objective Lung-Function Assessment

Relying solely on symptoms can lead to misdiagnosis. Objective tests confirm variable expiratory airflow limitation and document the baseline severity of the condition.

  • Spirometry with Bronchodilator Reversibility Testing: This is the preferred diagnostic test to confirm airflow limitation.
    • Obstructive Pattern: An initial FEV₁/FVC ratio below the lower limit of normal (typically <0.70 to 0.75 in adults) indicates expiratory airflow obstruction.
    • Positive Reversibility Criteria: After giving a short-acting bronchodilator (e.g., 4 puffs of albuterol), an increase in FEV₁ of >12% and >200 mL from the baseline confirms the diagnosis.
  • Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) Monitoring: Used when spirometry is unavailable or to track a patient’s daily control at home.
    • Diurnal Variability: Excess variability confirms asthma. Calculate this by measuring PEF twice daily (morning and night). An average daily diurnal PEF variability of >10% over 1–2 weeks supports the diagnosis.
  • Bronchial Provocation (Methacholine or Histamine Challenge): Reserved for patients with classic asthma symptoms but completely normal baseline spirometry.
    • Airway Hyperresponsiveness: A positive test is defined by a ≥20% drop in FEV₁ (PC₂₀) at a low concentration of the provoking agent, demonstrating hyperreactive airways.
  • Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO): A non-invasive biomarker test used to measure Type 2 (eosinophilic) airway inflammation.
    • Elevated FeNO levels (typically >25 ppb in adults or >20 ppb in children) support an allergic asthma profile and predict a favorable response to inhaled corticosteroids (ICS).

Individualized Stepwise Management Overview

Asthma pharmacotherapy should be adjusted dynamically in a stepwise cycle based on individual control levels, treatment tracks, and clinical review markers.

Step Level Track 1: Preferred Controller & Reliever (ICS-Formoterol) Track 2: Alternative Maintenance (SABA Reliever) Clinical Review Action
Step 1 As-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol for symptom relief. Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) taken whenever Short-Acting Beta₂-Agonist (SABA) is used. Confirm initial asthma diagnosis; check baseline lung function metrics.
Step 2 As-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol (same as Step 1). Daily regular low-dose ICS maintenance. Evaluate device-specific inhaler technique and optimize non-pharmacological trigger avoidance.
Step 3 Daily maintenance low-dose ICS-formoterol + as-needed reliever. Daily regular low-dose ICS-LABA combination maintenance. Thoroughly assess treatment adherence barriers before escalating step dosages.
Step 4 Daily maintenance medium-dose ICS-formoterol + as-needed reliever. Daily regular medium/high-dose ICS-LABA combination maintenance. Screen for and manage uncontrolled comorbidities (e.g., GERD, chronic rhinosinusitis).
Step 5 Add Long-Acting Muscarinic Antagonist (LAMA) or Biologic therapy; proceed to expert phenotyping. Add LAMA or consider specialized high-dose option matrix. Refer to a pulmonologist or respiratory specialist; evaluate eligibility for advanced therapies.

Patient educationExplain the diagnosis, purpose of treatment, adherence; (Taking Medication Correctly)The single most common cause of dangerous asthma flare-ups is poor adherence to controller medications.Preventer vs. Reliever: You must understand the distinct difference between your inhalers. Preventer inhalers (usually containing low-dose steroids) reduce swelling and must be taken every single day, even when you feel perfectly well. Reliever inhalers only relax the airway muscles during an attack and do not fix the underlying swelling.Inhaler Technique: Even the best medicine will fail if it does not reach your lungs. If using a Metered-Dose Inhaler (the classic puffer), using a valved holding chamber (spacer) dramatically increases the amount of medicine that enters your lungs while reducing throat irritation.Post-Inhaler Hygiene: Always rinse your mouth with water and spit it out after using any daily steroid-containing inhaler. This simple step prevents localized side effects like oral thrush (a fungal throat infection) or hoarseness., lifestyle measures; Trigger Identification and Avoidance: Track what sparks your symptoms. Common triggers include tobacco smoke, dust mites, strong perfumes, furry pets, mold, and cold air.Safe Physical Activity: Exercise is highly encouraged for overall health. If physical exertion triggers your asthma, taking your rescue inhaler 10 to 15 minutes before starting can completely prevent exercise-induced bronchoconstriction.Managing Comorbidities: Conditions like acid reflux (GERD) and chronic sinus allergies directly worsen asthma inflammation. Treating these conditions effectively helps stabilize your lungs and monitoring requirements and when to seek urgent care. Use teach-back to confirm understanding.

Clinical Framework: Patient-Centered Asthma Modifiers

This clinical framework provides immediate reference strategies for adjusting asthma treatment based on unique patient-centered modifiers and risk variables.

Patient Factor High-Risk Variable Preferred Clinical Strategy
Pregnancy Maternal hypoxia / Poor clinical control Prioritise Budesonide (ICS) and Albuterol (SABA). Do not withhold necessary controller step-ups; uncontrolled maternal asthma poses a far greater risk to the fetus than standard medications.
Advanced Age Poor manual dexterity / Cognitive decline Utilise MDIs paired with Valved Holding Chambers (Spacers) or breath-actuated devices. Conduct face-to-face technique reviews at every follow-up visit.
NSAID Sensitivity Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease (AERD) Strictly avoid Aspirin and all traditional NSAIDs. Use acetaminophen (paracetamol) for mild pre-natal or general pain relief. Clearly document cross-reactivity risks in the chart.
Cardiovascular Disease Beta-blocker induced bronchospasm Eliminate systemic beta-blockers (including ophthalmic drops) if they induce airway obstruction or blunt reliever efficacy. Transition to alternative agents like calcium channel blockers.
Financial Constraints Inability to purchase advanced inhalers Prescribe cost-effective options explicitly covered by local health formularies, national insurance schemes (e.g., SHIF/NHIF plans), or regional programs to minimize primary non-adherence.

Patient Checklist

  • Know the name and purpose of each medicine.
  • Know how and when to take or use treatment.
  • Ask what adverse effects require medical attention.
  • Keep appointments and monitoring tests.
  • Tell the healthcare team about OTC medicines, supplements and allergies.
  • Do not stop long-term treatment abruptly without professional advice unless an emergency clinician instructs otherwise.
