Clinical framework

What clinicians assess

This page organizes the major clinical decision points for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. It is designed for patient education and professional revision, not as a substitute for a current guideline, local protocol or individualized clinical assessment.

Diagnosis / assessment
Diagnostic Modality Diagnostic Criteria & Parameters Clinical Assessment & Management Impact
Glycemic Diagnostic Criteria
  • HbA1c: ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) measured using NGSP-certified assay.
  • Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG): ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (≥ 126 mg/dL) after at least 8 hours fasting.
  • 2-Hour OGTT: ≥ 11.1 mmol/L (≥ 200 mg/dL) during 75g Oral Glucose Tolerance Test.
  • Random Plasma Glucose: ≥ 11.1 mmol/L (≥ 200 mg/dL) in symptomatic patients (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss).
  • Requires two abnormal test results (either from same sample or two separate test occasions) in asymptomatic individuals[cite: 3].
  • Confirms T2DM baseline; guides initial choice between monotherapy vs. dual/combination antihyperglycemic therapy[cite: 3].
Renal Function & Albuminuria Assessment
  • eGFR: Calculate estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate using CKD-EPI formula[cite: 3].
  • Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (uACR): Spot morning urine test[cite: 3].
    • Normal: < 3 mg/mmol (< 30 mg/g)
    • Microalbuminuria: 3–30 mg/mmol (30–300 mg/g)
    • Macroalbuminuria: > 30 mg/mmol (> 300 mg/g)
  • Identifies Diabetic Kidney Disease (DKD)[cite: 3].
  • Directs initiation of renal-protective agents: SGLT2 inhibitors and ACE inhibitors / ARBs for cardiovascular and renal risk reduction[cite: 3].
Cardiovascular & Lipid Evaluation
  • Fasting Lipid Profile: Total Cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, and Triglycerides[cite: 3].
  • Blood Pressure: Office BP screening (≥ 130/80 mmHg threshold)[cite: 3].
  • ASCVD Risk Stratification: Evaluate existing Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (CAD, stroke, PAD) or high risk (hypertension, smoking, dyslipidemia)[cite: 3].
  • Establishes baseline cardiovascular risk profile[cite: 3].
  • Drives priority use of GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors independent of baseline HbA1c, alongside statin therapy[cite: 3].
End-Organ Complication Screening
  • Retinopathy Screening: Baseline dilated eye exam by an optometrist/ophthalmologist.
  • Neuropathy Screening: 10g monofilament test, pinprick sensation, and vibration sense (128 Hz tuning fork).
  • Foot Examination: Check skin integrity, calluses, structural deformities, and peripheral pulses (dorsalis pedis/posterior tibial).
  • Detects early microvascular complications at diagnosis.
  • Establishes baseline risk for diabetic foot ulcers, peripheral arterial disease, and diabetic retinopathy, driving preventive interventions.
Key monitoring
Monitoring Parameter Target Range & Monitoring Schedule Clinical Purpose & Action Thresholds
Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c)
  • Testing Frequency: Every 3 months if treatment is changing or glycemic targets are unmet; every 6 months if stable at target.
  • Standard Target: < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for most non-pregnant adults.
  • Individualized Targets: < 6.5% for young/low-risk patients; 7.5–8.0% for elderly/frail patients or high hypoglycemia risk.
  • Evaluates long-term glycemic control over the preceding 2–3 months.
  • Action Threshold: HbA1c above target requires treatment intensification (adding second/third-line agents or insulin)[cite: 3].
Self-Glucose Monitoring (SMBG / CGM)
  • Fasting Blood Glucose: 4.4–7.2 mmol/L (80–130 mg/dL)[cite: 3].
  • Postprandial (1–2h post-meal): < 10.0 mmol/L (< 180 mg/dL)[cite: 3].
  • Time in Range (CGM): Target > 70% of time between 3.9–10.0 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL).
  • Essential for patients on insulin, sulfonylureas, or with frequent hypoglycemia[cite: 3].
  • Guides real-time dose adjustments, meal planning, and physical activity management[cite: 3].
Renal Function & Electrolytes
  • eGFR & Urine ACR: Monitor annually; 2–4 times per year if CKD is present (eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m² or uACR > 3 mg/mmol)[cite: 3].
  • Serum Creatinine & Potassium: Check 1–2 weeks after initiating ACEi/ARB or SGLT2i.
  • Tracks progression of diabetic kidney disease and ensures drug safety[cite: 3].
  • Action: Adjust or stop Metformin if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²; adjust SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA dosing per specific renal thresholds[cite: 3].
Cardiovascular & Metabolic Profiling
  • Blood Pressure: Every clinical visit (Target: < 130/80 mmHg)[cite: 3].
  • Fasting Lipid Panel: Annually or every 4–12 weeks after statin dose adjustments[cite: 3].
  • Weight / BMI & Foot Check: Monthly to quarterly; daily self-foot inspection.
  • Prevents cardiovascular events and diabetic foot complications[cite: 3].
  • Action: Escalate antihypertensive/statin therapy if BP or LDL targets are not met[cite: 3].
Management principles
Management Category Standard Regimen & Dosing Framework Clinical Purpose & Indication Strategy
Lifestyle & Metabolic Intervention
  • Medical Nutrition Therapy: Fiber-rich, low-glycemic Mediterranean or DASH-style diet.
  • Physical Activity: At least 150 min/week moderate-intensity aerobic exercise plus 2–3 resistance sessions weekly.
  • Weight Management: Target 5–10% body weight loss in overweight/obese individuals.
  • Foundational intervention for all T2DM patients.
  • Improves insulin sensitivity, aids glycemic control, and reduces cardiovascular risk factors.
First-Line Foundation Therapy
  • Metformin: Start 500 mg daily/twice daily with meals; titrate up to 1000 mg twice daily as tolerated (or XR equivalent).
  • First-line agent for overall glycemic efficacy, weight neutrality, safety, and low hypoglycemia risk.
  • Dose-reduce if eGFR 30–45 mL/min/1.73m²; discontinue if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m².
Cardiorenal Risk-Driven Escalation
  • SGLT2 Inhibitors (Empagliflozin / Dapagliflozin): Dosed once daily (e.g., Empagliflozin 10–25 mg daily).
  • GLP-1 RAs (Dulaglutide / Semaglutide): Weekly subcutaneous administration titrated per protocol.
  • Indicated independent of baseline HbA1c or Metformin use in patients with established ASCVD, Heart Failure (HFrEF), or CKD (uACR > 3 mg/mmol).
  • Reduces HF hospitalizations, renal disease progression, and MACE events.
Glycemic Escalation & Advanced Therapy
  • Dual/Triple Oral Agents: Add DPP-4 inhibitors, Sulfonylureas (Gliclazide MR/Glimepiride), or Pioglitazone if HbA1c above target.
  • Basal Insulin: Initiate at 10 units daily or 0.1–0.2 units/kg/day (Glargine/Degludec) titrated to fasting glucose targets.
  • Indicated when HbA1c targets remain unmet.
  • Early insulin indicated for severe hyperglycemia (HbA1c > 10% / FPG > 16.7 mmol/L), symptomatic catabolism, or ketosis.
Follow-up / outcomes
Outcome Domain Evaluation Parameters & Schedule Target Outcomes & Action Thresholds
Glycemic Control & HbA1c Tracking
  • HbA1c Evaluation: Measure every 3 months until target achieved, then every 6 months.
  • Self-Monitored Blood Glucose (SMBG): Review glucose logs/CGM data at each visit.
  • Target: HbA1c < 7.0% (individualized) with fasting glucose 4.4–7.2 mmol/L.
  • Action: HbA1c > target warrants medication dose titration or therapy escalation.
Renal Function & Albuminuria Progress
  • eGFR & Urine ACR: Screen at least annually; every 3–6 months if baseline CKD or microalbuminuria.
  • Serum Potassium & Creatinine: Monitor periodically if taking ACEi/ARB or SGLT2i.
  • Target: Stable eGFR and reduction/stabilization of uACR (< 3 mg/mmol).
  • Action: Rapid eGFR decline or worsening albuminuria indicates need for nephrology consultation and optimization of cardiorenal agents.
Weight Management & Hypoglycemia Risk
  • Body Weight & BMI: Measure at every follow-up visit.
  • Hypoglycemia Log: Evaluate frequency, severity, and timing of blood glucose < 3.9 mmol/L (< 70 mg/dL).
  • Target: 5–10% weight loss target for overweight/obese patients; zero severe hypoglycemic events.
  • Action: Recurring hypoglycemia requires reduction/discontinuation of sulfonylureas or insulin, or switching to weight-neutral/low-hypo risk classes (SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA).
Complication & Cardiovascular Screening
  • Blood Pressure & Lipids: BP every visit; lipid panel annually.
  • Retinal & Foot Screening: Comprehensive dilated eye exam and monofilament foot exam annually.
  • Target: BP < 130/80 mmHg; LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (< 70 mg/dL); absence of foot ulcers or proliferative retinopathy.
  • Action: Intensify lipid/hypertension management or prompt referral to ophthalmology/podiatry upon abnormal findings.
Patient education
Education Domain Core Clinical Explanation & Guidance Teach-Back Prompts & Verification
Diagnosis & Purpose of Treatment
  • Understanding Diabetes: Explain that T2DM occurs when the body doesn't produce enough insulin or use it effectively, causing sugar to build up in the blood.
  • Goal of Therapy: Reassure the patient that managing blood sugar prevents long-term damage to the heart, eyes, kidneys, and nerves.
  • Long-Term Commitment: Emphasize that medication and lifestyle changes work together as a lifelong management strategy.
  • "In your own words, why is keeping your blood sugar in the target range important for your long-term health?"
  • "How would you explain what Type 2 Diabetes is to a family member?"
Medication Adherence & Safety
  • Consistent Dosing: Stress taking medications exactly as prescribed (e.g., Metformin with meals to reduce stomach upset).
  • Recognizing Hypoglycemia: Teach symptoms of low blood sugar (shakiness, sweating, confusion, dizziness, fast heartbeat).
  • Rule of 15: Treat glucose < 3.9 mmol/L (< 70 mg/dL) with 15g fast-acting carbohydrate (e.g., 1/2 cup juice or 3–4 glucose tablets), wait 15 minutes, and recheck.
  • "What signs would tell you that your blood sugar is dropping too low, and what steps will you take immediately?"
  • "When and how should you take your daily diabetes medications?"
Lifestyle Measures & Foot Care
  • Nutrition & Activity: Guide on balanced meal proportions (plate method), reducing sugary drinks, and staying physically active daily.
  • Daily Foot Inspection: Inspect feet every day for cuts, blisters, redness, or swelling; keep feet dry and wear comfortable, fitted shoes.
  • Sick-Day Rules: Continue taking basal insulin/medications, stay hydrated, check glucose more frequently, and call the clinic if unable to keep food down.
  • "What is your plan for checking your feet every day at home?"
  • "What changes can you make to your daily meals to help manage your blood sugar levels?"
Monitoring & When to Seek Urgent Care
  • Self-Monitoring: Show how to record blood glucose readings and bring the logbook/device to every appointment.
  • Regular Checks: Remind the patient about scheduled HbA1c lab tests every 3–6 months and annual eye and kidney exams.
  • Urgent Red Flags:
    • Persistent high glucose with nausea, severe thirst, or confusion (DKA/HHS risk)
    • Non-healing foot ulcers or sores
    • Sudden changes in vision
    • Chest pain or shortness of breath
  • "What warning signs or symptoms mean you should seek medical help right away?"
  • "How often do you need to come back for your blood tests and routine check-ups?"
Multidisciplinary roles
Healthcare Discipline Core Responsibilities & Interventions Collaborative Synergy & Clinical Goals
Physicians / Endocrinologists
  • Diagnosis & Risk Assessment: Confirm T2DM diagnosis, evaluate baseline organ function, and establish individual HbA1c targets.
  • Treatment Planning: Prescribe and escalate glucose-lowering therapies (e.g., SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA, Insulin) based on cardiorenal risks.
  • Leads clinical decision-making, coordinates multi-organ risk reduction, and manages complex endocrine/metabolic complications.
Clinical Pharmacists
  • Medication Optimization: Assess renal dose adjustments, evaluate potential drug-drug interactions, and recommend cardiorenal-protective agents.
  • Adherence Support: Identify medication side effects (e.g., GI distress with Metformin) and provide targeted adherence counseling.
  • Optimizes drug regimens, prevents adverse drug events, and improves long-term therapeutic adherence.
Diabetes Educators & Nurses
  • DSMES Delivery: Provide comprehensive Diabetes Self-Management Education and Support (glucometer use, insulin injection techniques, sick-day rules).
  • Routine Surveillance: Perform screening (monofilament foot checks, BP monitoring) and facilitate continuous care coordination.
  • Empowers self-care management, bridges routine patient monitoring, and ensures early identification of complications.
Dietitians, Podiatrists & Specialists
  • Nutritional & Specialized Care: Formulate tailored meal strategies, provide high-risk foot care/ulcer management, and conduct annual dilated eye exams.
  • Lifestyle Support: Guide physical activity plans and weight loss strategies.
  • Mitigates microvascular risks (retinopathy, neuropathy, foot ulcers) and supports sustained lifestyle modification.
Individualization
Individualization Variable Clinical Assessment & Risk Modifiers Tailored Management Adjustments
Age & Health Status / Frailty
  • Older Adults (≥ 65 yrs) & Frail Patients: High vulnerability to hypoglycemia, falls, cognitive impairment, and drug interactions.
  • Young Adults: Long life expectancy; aggressive glycemic control prevents cumulative micro/macrovascular complications.
  • Relax HbA1c targets to 7.5%–8.0% (or higher in severe frailty); prioritize non-hypoglycemic agents (SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i). Avoid sulfonylureas.
  • Maintain tighter targets (< 6.5%–7.0%) in young adults without severe hypoglycemia history.
Pregnancy & Gestational Intent
  • Teratogenicity: Most oral glucose-lowering agents (SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i, Statins, ACEi/ARBs) are contraindicated in pregnancy.
  • Uncontrolled Glycemia: Elevates congenital malformation and pregnancy loss risks.
  • Transition to Insulin prior to conception or immediately upon pregnancy confirmation; Metformin may be maintained in select cases under expert care.
  • Enforce strict glycemic targets (HbA1c < 6.0%–6.5%) during pregnancy.
Renal & Hepatic Impairment
  • Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD): Reduced drug clearance increases risk of lactic acidosis (Metformin) and severe hypoglycemia.
  • Hepatic Insufficiency: Impaired gluconeogenesis increases hypoglycemia risk; altered drug metabolism.
  • Dose-reduce Metformin if eGFR < 45; stop if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m². Initiate SGLT2i for kidney protection down to eGFR thresholds per drug labeling.
  • Select agents with hepatic-safe profiles (e.g., Linagliptin requires no renal/hepatic adjustment).
Comorbidities & Polypharmacy
  • High Cardiorenal Risk: Co-existing ASCVD, Heart Failure, or diabetic nephropathy.
  • Polypharmacy: Drug interactions (e.g., corticosteroids worsening hyperglycemia, beta-blockers masking hypoglycemia).
  • Prioritize SGLT2 inhibitors and/or GLP-1 RAs regardless of baseline HbA1c.
  • Simplify regimens; adjust blood glucose monitoring frequency when starting systemic steroids.
Socioeconomic Barriers & Preferences
  • Financial Constraints: Cost of newer agents (GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i) and testing supplies (strips, CGMs).
  • Patient Preference & Lifestyle: Resistance to daily injections, irregular meal patterns, or shift work.
  • Utilize cost-effective options (generic Metformin, low-cost Sulfonylureas, Human Insulins) when cost is a restrictive barrier.
  • Engage in shared decision-making to align therapeutic choices (oral vs. injectable) with patient routines.

Patient Checklist

  • Know the name and purpose of each medicine.
  • Know how and when to take or use treatment.
  • Ask what adverse effects require medical attention.
  • Keep appointments and monitoring tests.
  • Tell the healthcare team about OTC medicines, supplements and allergies.
  • Do not stop long-term treatment abruptly without professional advice unless an emergency clinician instructs otherwise.

🚨 When urgent medical care may be needed

Altered consciousness, severe dehydration, persistent vomiting, suspected DKA/HHS
